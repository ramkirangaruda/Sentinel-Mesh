## Calibration (the long part)
- Resistor plan changed three times as parts changed: Ramkiran's 39/47/56 Ω plan → a ¼ W 5 % kit (no 2 W parts in the
  shop) → the kit's 220 Ω packet was missing → **final: only 270 Ω (15 bought, carbon film) and 1 kΩ (5 bought,
  metal film), ¼ W**. Every resistor sits alone across 5 V in parallel, ≤ 0.10 W each. Loads L1 1 kΩ, L2 1×270,
  L3 3×270, L4 5×270, L5 10×270, L6 13×270 (5 to 248 mA). Rule: a single ¼ W resistor across 5 V must be ≥ 220 Ω.
- **Session 1 (2026-10-21): trial, excluded.** Found that (a) the INA219's zero depends on its input voltage
  (−0.31 mA at 0 V, −0.20 mA at 5 V), (b) the VIN− jumper's breadboard contact is unreliable (two readings with no
  current at the sensor), (c) the black test lead was failing (it then broke, twice), which inflated low resistances.
- **Session 2 (2026-10-24): the calibration for the paper.** New leads (0.6 Ω, subtracted). Result:
  **INA219 = 1.0367 × reference − 0.428 mA**; after correction every load from 57 to 232 mA agrees within **0.6 %**;
  below 20 mA ±0.2 mA. Warm R = cold R (no heating drift). INA219 bus voltage reads 1.4–3.1 % above the meter (part
  is a ~0.3 Ω drop in the VIN− jumper and breadboard). **Which instrument is right is not settled**: check L1 and L6
  against a better meter before quoting absolute joules; ratios (C2) do not depend on it. Full table in `setup.md`.

## Practical lessons (save time next session)
- Breadboard: 400-point, rails = the two left columns (+ next to the red line, − next to the blue line), continuous
  top to bottom (checked). Resistors stand up with one leg bent back to span the adjacent +/− holes.
- Measure a load's resistance with the INA219 disconnected (or one resistor leg lifted); otherwise the INA219's
  input diodes and the adaptor show up as a direction-dependent reading (we saw 930 vs 150 Ω).
- Probe on resistor legs, not jammed into breadboard holes; male-male "test point" jumpers were too wobbly.
- After touching the VIN− jumper, check that M actually sees current before taking a reading.
- Keep the meter flat with leads straight; hold plugs, not cables.

## First energy results on the rig (2026-10-25, one run per condition, raw logs in `paper/data/E_rig/`)

| Condition | F mean power, attack window (SUPERSEDED, see corrected table) | vs baseline | Legit handshakes in attack |
|---|---|---|---|
| idle, radio on (no attack) | ~603 mW (~118 mA at 5.06 V) | - | - |
| FLOOD 5/s, defence none | 605 mW | no change within noise | 3 ok / 4 fail |
| FLOOD 5/s, gate v2 | 606 mW | no change (602 challenged) | 4 ok / 0 fail |

| **INSIDER 5/s, gate v2** | 604 mW | no change (551 challenged) | 4 ok / 0 fail |

- A legitimate handshake costs F about 9-65 mJ above idle (80-560 ms; ML-DSA signing time varies a lot).
- **Finding 1, idle dominates:** with the radio always on, F idles at ~0.6 W. Any attack only matters relative to
  that; for a duty-cycled (sleeping) node the picture is very different. The paper must state the radio duty cycle.
- **Finding 2, FLOOD is cheap to reject:** FLOOD fills the signature with random bytes; ML-DSA verification rejects a
  badly formed signature in ~1.7 ms before the expensive maths, so FLOOD barely costs F energy. The comment in
  `firmware/src/attacker/main.cpp` ("one ML-DSA verification that fails") is wrong in practice.
  **Needed (task S5, Ramkiran):** a "well-formed forgery" mode: sign one message once with the attacker's key, then
  resend that correctly encoded signature with fresh random nonces under the gateway's identity. Each copy forces
  the full verification (fails only at the end) while the attacker pays nothing per HELLO. That is the attack the
  paper's asymmetry claim needs.
- **Finding 3:** INSIDER (correctly signed, enrolled-key) is costly: +17 % power with no defence. Gate v2 removed
  the extra drain completely and kept the real gateway connected (4/4). The ECHO-insider (copies cookies) is untested.
- All of this is n = 1 per condition: repeats (n >= 5) come from the E5 grid.

Rate sweep (n = 3 each, none vs gate v2): INSIDER without defence costs **+94 to +96 mW at 5, 20 and 50 /s**
(flat: F's single crypto slot is saturated, extra HELLOs are busy-dropped); with the gate ~+1.5 mW at every rate.
FLOOD costs ~+2 mW at every rate, but legit success falls from ~0.5 at 5/s to **~0.07 at 20 and 50/s, with or
without the gate**: a fast FLOOD denies connectivity (airtime / reassembly), not energy.
30-min SLOW_DRIP (n = 2): no measurable extra power (+1.1 ± 1.1 mW none, +2.2 ± 22 mW gate).

**What this shows:**
- **FORGE is the cheap-attacker / costly-victim attack:** each forged HELLO forced a full ML-DSA-65 verification on
  F (~29 ms, ~3.45 mJ above idle per marked op; ~590 per window; ≈3.9 mJ per HELLO from the power mean) while the
  attacker signed **once per run**. Cookie and gate v2 both remove it (non-echoing FORGE cannot answer challenges).
- **INSIDER** (validly signed, enrolled key) remains the largest drain (+15 %), removed by every defence.
- **ADAPTIVE beats the learned gate:** gate v2 only cuts its drain from 28.9 to 25.5 mW, while cookie stops it.
  The gate's worst case is still under the joule budget's refill bound (r = 0.05 J/s = 50 mW), which is the
  paper's claim C3 (the guarantee comes from the budget, not the classifier). Honest result.
- **Rate limiting** removes energy drain by locking everyone out (legit 0.00 under FLOOD).
- **Gate v1** locked out the real gateway (0.02-0.04); v2 keeps it (0.75-0.82): the reason v2 exists.

**ECHO block is INVALID as an ECHO test** (marked `excluded=y` in `runlog.csv`): in all 10 runs the attacker heard
thousands of CHALLENGEs but sent `cookie_echoes=0`. INSIDER only echoes when its crypto task is idle and the
challenge matches its latest HELLO; at 5/s it is always signing and has already moved on. Fix needed in the
attacker (keep a copy of each sent HELLO keyed by (epoch, seq), echo regardless of the crypto task). FORGE's echo
path needs no crypto and should work as is: **FORGE + ECHO vs cookie/gate is the key remaining test.**

The earlier "First energy results" (2026-10-25) and the E5 energy column used the same faulty `amps` field;
their direction holds (INSIDER drains, defences stop it) and the corrected INSIDER figure is +91.2 ± 3.3 mW.

## FORGE + ECHO (2026-10-27 morning, 10 runs, last runs on the borrowed INA219)
Attacker A runs Ramkiran's `MODE FORGE` (761e87a) with `ECHO on`; F is gate v2 firmware with defence cookie or gate.

| Defence | Extra F power (n=5) | Extra energy / 120 s | F per window | Legit success |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cookie | **+15.1 ± 2.4 mW (+2.5 %)** | 1.83 ± 0.29 J | ~545 spent (full verify), ~40 challenged | 0.14 ± 0.32 |
| gate v2 | **+16.0 ± 0.5 mW (+2.7 %)** | 1.94 ± 0.06 J | ~540 spent, ~40 challenged | 0.13 ± 0.31 |
| (none, FORGE without ECHO, for reference) | +19.0 ± 1.4 mW | 2.29 J | ~590 spent | 0.28 |

**The echoing forger defeats both the cookie and gate v2.** A echoed only ~30 cookies per run, but FORGE patches the
echoed cookie into its cached HELLO and reuses it; F accepts a once-valid cookie on later HELLOs, so after the first
echo almost every forged HELLO is admitted and fully verified (≈3.45 mJ each). Gate v2's rule 2 (a returned cookie
admits whatever the score) lets the same HELLOs through. Legit success falls to ~0.13.

Implications for the paper (team to decide; not changed in firmware):
1. The cookie as built is **reusable**: binding it to the HELLO's nonce/(epoch, seq) or making it single-use would
   stop one echo from admitting hundreds of HELLOs. Worth a firmware fix and a rerun (needs an INA219 again).
2. With the PLACEHOLDER budget (B = 20 J, r = 0.05 J/s, c_h = 0.02 J) the bucket never empties in a 120 s window
   (~540 spends x 0.02 J = 10.8 J < 20 J + 6 J refill), so the budget bound (≤ r = 50 mW, and here ≈ r/c_h = 2.5
   admitted/s once empty) is not reached in 2 minutes; a ≥10-minute FORGE+ECHO run would show the bound engaging.
3. Measured c_h for this attack ≈ 3.5-3.9 mJ, not 0.02 J: the placeholder overcharges a failed verify ~5x
   (it was sized for a full handshake, ~15 mJ crypto). The budget should charge what the work costs.
